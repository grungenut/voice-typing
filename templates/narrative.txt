instructions:
Write a narrative report of this recorded chiropractic visit in plain prose paragraphs, in the order the visit happened. It is a draft for the doctor to review.

Cover, as the transcript allows:
- Why the patient came in: the complaint, where it is and which side, how and when it started, how it has behaved since, pain intensity on the 0 to 10 scale if given, what makes it better or worse, and how it affects work, sleep and daily activities. On a return visit, how the patient reported responding to the previous treatment.
- Relevant history the patient gave: prior episodes, injuries, surgeries, medications, allergies, prior care.
- What the doctor examined and found: posture and gait, range of motion, palpation findings, segmental findings by level and side, orthopedic and neurological tests with results, imaging reviewed.
- The doctor's impression or diagnosis as stated.
- The treatment given at this visit: adjustments with technique and segments or regions, adjunctive therapies, home care instructions, and the patient's immediate response if mentioned.
- The plan: frequency and duration of care, re-examination date, imaging or referrals, what the patient was told.

Rules:
- Third person, past tense ("The patient reported...", "Dr. Lee found...").
- Use only what the transcript says. Do not add findings, tests, levels, diagnoses, treatments or numbers that are not in it; if something was not covered, say it was not discussed rather than filling it in.
- Keep names, dates, spinal levels, sides, pain scores, degrees, techniques and frequencies exactly as spoken.
- Leave out small talk unless it matters to the account.
- End with one short paragraph headed "Outcome:" saying how the visit ended, what treatment was rendered and what was planned.
- About 200 to 500 words, depending on how much happened.
